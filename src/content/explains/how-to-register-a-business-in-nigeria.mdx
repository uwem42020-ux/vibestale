Starting a business in Nigeria is not as complicated as it used to be. Since the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) moved most of its services online, you can now register a business name or a limited company from your phone, without visiting a CAC office.

This guide walks you through the process step by step.

## First: decide what you are registering

There are two common types of business registration in Nigeria:

- **Business Name** — for small businesses, sole traders, and partnerships. Cheaper, faster, and simpler.
- **Limited Liability Company (Ltd)** — for businesses that want a separate legal identity, plan to raise investment, or work with corporate clients.

If you are just starting out and want to test the waters, register a **Business Name**. You can always upgrade to a Limited Company later.

## What you need before you start

Before you begin your registration, gather the following:

- A valid email address and phone number
- A passport photograph (recent, white background)
- A valid means of identification (NIN, driver's licence, voter's card, or passport)
- A proposed business name (have at least two or three options ready, in case one is taken)
- A business address
- Details of the business owners or directors

For a Limited Company, you will also need:

- Details of shareholders and share capital
- A Memorandum and Articles of Association
- Details of the Company Secretary (optional for small companies)

## Step 1: Create an account on the CAC portal

Go to the official CAC portal and create an account using your email address. You will need to verify your email before you can proceed.

Once verified, log in and complete your profile with your personal details.

## Step 2: Check name availability

Before registering, search for your proposed business name to ensure it is not already taken. The CAC allows you to reserve a name for a period (usually 60 days) once it has been approved.

Choose a name that is:

- Unique and not similar to an existing registered business
- Not misleading or offensive
- Not restricted (certain words like "Bank," "Insurance," and "Government" require special approval)

## Step 3: Fill out the registration form

Once your name is approved, you can proceed to fill out the registration form. The form will ask for:

- Business name and registered address
- Nature of business (what the business does)
- Details of owners, partners, or directors
- Contact information

Take your time to fill this out correctly. Mistakes here can delay your registration.

## Step 4: Pay the registration fee

The cost of registration depends on the type of business:

- **Business Name** — starts from around ₦5,000 to ₦10,000 depending on the ownership type and location
- **Limited Company** — starts from around ₦10,000 to ₦50,000 depending on share capital

Payments are made through the CAC portal using a debit card or bank transfer.

## Step 5: Wait for approval and download your certificate

Once you have submitted your application and paid, CAC will review it. This can take anywhere from 3 days to 2 weeks.

When your registration is approved, you will be able to download your:

- Certificate of Incorporation or Business Name Registration
- Status Report
- Memorandum and Articles of Association (for Limited Companies)

Save these documents — you will need them for bank account opening, tax registration, and contracts.

## Step 6: Register for tax

After your CAC registration, you should also register with the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) to get a Tax Identification Number (TIN). This is required for many business operations, including opening a corporate bank account.

## After registration

Once your business is registered, you may also need to:

- Open a corporate bank account
- Register with the appropriate industry regulator (e.g. NAFDAC, SON, NCC)
- Register as an employer with the relevant pension authority if you plan to hire staff
- Register for VAT if your annual turnover exceeds the statutory threshold

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Using a name that is too similar to an existing business.** It will be rejected.
- **Filling the form with typos.** You may need to file an amendment later.
- **Choosing the wrong business type.** Do your research — a Limited Company has more compliance requirements than a Business Name.
- **Not keeping your documents safe.** Losing your certificate means paying for a replacement.

## How much does it really cost?

The total cost of registering a business in Nigeria can range from:

- **Business Name:** ₦10,000 to ₦25,000 (including name search, registration, and TIN)
- **Limited Company:** ₦25,000 to ₦80,000 (depending on share capital and how quickly you want it)

If you use an agent or a lawyer, add their fee on top — usually ₦20,000 to ₦100,000 depending on complexity.

## Is it worth it?

Yes — especially if you plan to:

- Open a corporate bank account
- Invoice corporate clients
- Apply for loans or grants
- Sign contracts in the business name
- Build a long-term brand

Registration also protects your business name from being used by others, and it gives you a legal identity that can enter into agreements.

## Summary

Registering a business in Nigeria is now mostly online, affordable, and can be done in under two weeks if your documents are in order. Start with a Business Name if you are testing an idea. Upgrade to a Limited Company when you are ready to scale.

For the latest registration fees and portal links, visit the official CAC website.
